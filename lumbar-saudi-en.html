01 / SAUDI CONTEXT

High prevalence, a new guideline, uneven practice.

Low back pain is the leading cause of disability in Saudi Arabia, according to the national guideline itself. The 2026 guideline structures assessment and management, but leaves exercise prescription open.

NATIONAL GUIDELINE 2026

Saudi clinical practice guideline for low back pain and sciatica in adults

Aldera M, … Alshehri MA. J Clin Med 2026;15(2):528. Developed by a multidisciplinary task force with the NCEBM of the Saudi Health Council using GRADE ADOLOPMENT, with NICE NG59 as the primary source. It answers 11 clinical questions and adds one on pain neuroscience education.

TopicStrengthContent
Validated risk toolsStrongStratify with tools such as STarT Back (very low certainty).
Stratified managementStrongMatch treatment intensity to risk (moderate certainty).
ImagingConditionalOnly with red flags or if the result would change management.
Other recommendationsConditionalDrugs for sciatica, psychological interventions, multidisciplinary return to work, epidural injections, decompression, radiofrequency denervation and pain neuroscience education (very low to low certainty).
Exercise doseNot addressedNone of the 11 questions covers exercise frequency, volume, intensity or progression.
Full text on PMC (PMC12842004)
Watch the NICE update. In July 2026, after the Saudi adaptation, NICE withdrew the NG59 recommendations on psychological therapy and on combined physical and psychological programmes. The Saudi recommendations derived from them are worth reviewing. NICE NG59, update information
PREVALENCE

Meta-analysis of 124 Saudi studies with 146,552 participants: annual prevalence 52.3% and lifetime prevalence 64.0%.

doi.org/10.2147/jpr.s607375

How it is practised: three surveys

2021 · Int J Gen MedAlhowimel et al.35%

Of 72 primary care physicians working in Saudi Arabia, only 35% reported using clinical practice guidelines for low back pain. Orientation was mostly biomedical.

2022 · Physiother Theory PractMoslem et al. abstract only5.3% · 57.4%

133 physiotherapists answered 6 clinical vignettes. Guideline adherence ranged from 5.3% to 57.4%: highest with red flags, lowest in acute pain with mobility deficit.

304 physiotherapists. Most used treatments: home exercise 87.1%, education 82.0%, specific back exercises 80.6% and electrotherapy 61.9%. Passive treatments not recommended by guidelines remain common.

02 / INTERNATIONAL GUIDELINES

All recommend exercise. Almost none say how much.

Eight guidelines reviewed. Only the Dutch KNGF 2021 and the Danish draft give figures for frequency, duration or intensity; WHO sets a minimum of total hours.

NOT A CLINICAL GUIDELINE

The McGill approach

Stuart McGill, a spine biomechanist (Spine Biomechanics Laboratory, University of Waterloo), is a highly influential author in practice. His approach is an author's proposal, not a guideline recommendation.

Verifiable example: McGill and Karpowicz 2009 used surface EMG and 3D analysis to quantify variants of the curl up, side bridge and bird dog in 8 healthy men, to guide starting level, progression, dosage and corrective technique. These are the three exercises popularly called the “Big 3”. It is basic science, not a patient trial.

McGill SM, Karpowicz A. Arch Phys Med Rehabil 2009;90(1):118-26
03 / DOSE EVIDENCE

Dose response: flat, U shaped and low certainty.

The best analyses predict useful ranges, but none shows a single optimal dose. Individualisation, supervision and adherence matter as much as volume.

Arora et al. · BJSM 2026 abstract only

239 RCTs, dose-response network meta-analysis

Predicted optimal doses in the short term:

Disability
4 sessions/wk25 min13 weeks
Pain
3 sessions/wk30 min8 weeks

Credible intervals overlap, there is little additional benefit with higher exposure, 84% of trials are at high risk of bias and certainty is low or very low. The authors conclude that a single optimal dose is not supported.

04 / GAPS AND FUTURE RESEARCH

What still needs measuring in Riyadh.

The proposals in this section are working ideas, not results.

GAP 1

The 2026 Saudi guideline does not address exercise dose, and the international evidence on dose is low certainty.

GAP 2

There is no open chronic low back pain dataset with session-level load (RPE, time under tension, progression). The closest are CFT (sessions and minutes) and Neason (on request).

GAP 3

There are no data on adherence to the 2026 guideline. The available Saudi surveys date from 2020 to 2022, before the guideline.

Proposed study: adherence to the 2026 Saudi guideline PROPOSAL

A cross-sectional vignette survey of physiotherapists and primary care physicians in Riyadh, following the designs of Moslem et al. 2022 and Alhowimel et al. 2021.

1 · Design

Vignettes on STarT Back stratification, imaging decisions and exercise prescription.

2 · Measurement

Share of answers concordant with the 2026 guideline and what exercise dose is prescribed in practice.

3 · Use

Identify training needs and contribute local data to the dose gap.

CONCEPT, NOT VALIDATED

Rule-based dosing protocol

A rule set derived from guidelines and meta-analyses for a rule engine like the Achilles module. It has not been tested in patients and does not replace clinical judgement.

  1. 2 to 4 sessions per week.Arora 2026; Denmark (draft); KNGF
  2. 25 to 45 minutes per session.Arora 2026 (25 to 30 min); Mueller 2020 (20 to 30 min); Denmark allows up to 90 min
  3. 8 to 13 weeks, with at least 20 h of total programme time.Arora 2026; WHO 2023
  4. Strength per the KNGF table: 60 to 70% of 1RM, 2 to 4 sets of 8 to 12.KNGF 2021
  5. Time contingent progression, not pain contingent, with the 24 h response rule.KNGF 2021
  6. Alert if frequency falls below about 1.5 sessions per week.Association with recurrence in a small cohort (Krause 2021, abstract only); exploratory threshold
  7. Record session RPE, attendance and NRS every 2 weeks.Variables used by Neason 2024 and Iqbal

Personalise, supervise and support adherence (WHO 2023, Hayden 2005).
